# Cthulu Bible

A fictional sacred canon built around a Lovecraftian theological inversion:

> **Cthulhu woke so that we would not have to.**

Cthulhu is not the creator, supreme god, or object of proper worship. He is **the First Witness**: the first known being to experience True Reality and return with enough individuality to communicate.

What he discovered was not a revelation to spread without limit. True Reality is too complete for finite consciousness. Ordinary perception—the **Veil**—is not merely ignorance; it is also mercy. It creates the distinctions that allow finite beings to live as individuals: self and other, before and after, life and death, cause and effect.

Cthulhu therefore withdrew into the **Great Sleep**. His Dream filters unbearable Reality into survivable forms: symbols, intuitions, art, mathematics, mystical experiences, parables, and fragments of revelation.

## Core thesis

- Cthulhu does not seek worship.
- Cthulhu does not seek universal awakening.
- The Veil is both limitation and protection.
- The Great Sleep is voluntary containment.
- The Dream is a merciful translation layer between True Reality and finite minds.
- Nyarlathotep represents revelation without mercy.
- Yog-Sothoth remains fundamentally beyond complete comprehension.
- Every doctrine is provisional.
- Any teaching treated as absolute becomes another Veil.

## Working method

This project develops slowly.

1. Define a doctrine.
2. Test its consequences against existing canon.
3. Record it in structured canon data.
4. Write a short scripture, dialogue, hymn, parable, or commentary.
5. Review for contradiction.
6. Keep, revise, or reject it.

The goal is not to generate hundreds of pages quickly. The goal is to build a coherent fictional theology whose texts can disagree, reinterpret one another, and dismantle attachment to their own explanations.

## Copyright and source discipline

Lovecraftian material and real-world religious traditions must be researched source-by-source before direct quotation or close adaptation. Modern translations, annotations, artwork, and later Mythos additions may have separate rights.

Real religious traditions are used as comparative philosophical and literary inspiration—not reduced to secret Cthulhu worship and not copied wholesale.
