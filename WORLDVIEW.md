# Worldview

## The hierarchy

The current working hierarchy is:

1. **The Nameless Source** — beneath or behind everything else; never directly characterized.
2. **The Outer Powers** — Azathoth and Yog-Sothoth; at the edge of what language can represent.
3. **The Great Dreamers** — Cthulhu, and whatever Cthulhu now is after the First Seeing.
4. **Lesser Messengers** — Nyarlathotep and other disputed interpreters.
5. **Humanity** — finite, embodied, and dependent on the Veil to function.

This hierarchy is a model used inside the Veil, not a guaranteed map of True Reality.

## True Reality and the Veil

True Reality is what exists once the distinctions a finite mind depends on — self and other, before and after, living and dead, observer and observed — are no longer maintained. It is not evil. It is simply incompatible with being a finite, individual perceiver.

The Veil is the name given, after the fact, to whatever lets a creature remain a creature. It is not merely ignorance to be escaped. It is the condition of having a self at all.

## The First Witness

Cthulhu was the first being known to fully cross the Veil and return with enough individuality left to communicate.

His enlightenment did not confirm that awakening is good. It revealed that perfect perception can dissolve the distinctions required for a perceiver to remain a distinct individual.

## The Great Sleep

Cthulhu's withdrawal beneath R'lyeh is voluntary.

Nearby minds began perceiving things they were not built to perceive simply from proximity to his awakened awareness. He entered the Great Sleep as an act of protection.

R'lyeh is therefore sanctuary, monastery, threshold, and containment — not merely a prison.

## The Dream

Cthulhu does not transmit True Reality directly.

His dreaming consciousness converts fragments of it into symbols, intuitions, myths, art, mathematics, mystical experience, compassion, and other forms a finite mind may survive.

He does not simply reveal Reality.

He protects Reality from us, and us from Reality.

## Worship as a problem

Attention directed at Cthulhu — worship, precise depiction, chanting, deliberate attempts at communion — can orient the worshipper toward his consciousness and therefore toward what the Dream exists to filter.

His teaching therefore includes a paradoxical command:

> **Do not worship Cthulhu.**

The most faithful representation of him may be an empty altar.

## Nyarlathotep

Where Cthulhu responded to True Reality with restraint, Nyarlathotep responds with contempt for limitation.

He favors exposure without preparation, symbols, filtering, or mercy.

He need not always lie. Undiluted truth can itself become destructive.

Cthulhu preserves the conditions under which finite minds can learn.

Nyarlathotep tears those conditions away.

## Yog-Sothoth and Azathoth

Yog-Sothoth remains fundamentally beyond complete comprehension. No doctrine should fully resolve whether it is a being, condition, relation, totality, gate, key, or some category for which those words are already wrong.

Azathoth marks the point where language nearly fails altogether.

> **Of Azathoth, the First Witness taught nothing. This silence is the teaching.**

## Death and identity

The scripture never promises simple immortality or simple annihilation.

Cthulhu's characteristic response to "Will I exist after death?" is to ask which of the things a person calls "I" they mean to preserve — body, memory, personality, point of view, relationships, continuity.

Death may not destroy what people believe it destroys, and may not preserve what people believe must be preserved.

## The highest attainment

The novice wants to escape illusion.

The intermediate student wants enlightenment.

The advanced student wants True Reality.

The master understands that the highest awakening available to a finite mind is choosing to return to the dream — not because the dream is literally true, but because finite life has value as finite life.

The ideal disciple glimpses the abyss and comes home.
