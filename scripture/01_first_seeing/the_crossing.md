# The Crossing

The Witness waited until the tide began to return.

Water entered the chamber by the lower stones and spread across the floor without haste. It reached the first line, erased what remained of it, and continued.

At the higher line the Witness lowered one foot.

For a moment the chalk remained between his feet.

Then he stepped across.

Nothing opened.

No voice spoke.

The wall remained stone. The water remained cold around his ankles. Far above, a gull called once and was silent.

The Witness stood waiting for the boundary to answer him.

It did not.

So he closed his eyes.

He let the sound of the water come without deciding first whether it was near or far.

He let the cold arrive without asking where the cold ended and the one who felt it began.

He let the next breath enter without naming the moment before it or the moment after.

The chamber did not disappear.

It ceased, for an instant, to require a chamber.

The water did not become something else.

It ceased, for an instant, to require a thing that was water and another thing that was touched by it.

The Witness reached for the line behind him.

He remembered drawing it.

He remembered the hand that had drawn it.

He could not discover why those memories should belong to the same hand.

Then the gull called again.

The sound was not above him.

It was not after the first call.

It was not his to hear.

And still, somewhere that could be called a stair only by mercy, a clay cup waited beside a name.

The Witness tried to turn toward it.

There was no direction in which to turn.
