# The Mark in Chalk

The next mark was made in chalk.

A woman drew it on the harbor wall before dawn: a circle, a line, and beneath it three small strokes.

The keeper found her there with white dust on her fingers.

"He said the marks were false," he told her.

"Did he?"

The keeper hesitated.

She pointed to the three strokes.

"These are my sons. When the eldest sails, I touch this one. When the youngest is angry, I touch this one. The middle one never remembers his coat."

"And the circle?"

"I don't know. It was already being drawn when I began."

The keeper went back to the house and told the Witness.

"Should I wash it away?"

The Witness asked, "Can the rain?"

"Yes."

"Then leave the rain its work."

That afternoon the woman returned with her youngest son. He added a crooked fourth stroke and laughed when she protested.

That night it rained.

By morning the circle had become a pale curve. Two strokes remained. The others had run down the wall in thin white threads.

The woman stood before it for a long while.

Then she drew three new strokes beside what remained.

She did not redraw the circle.

The keeper watched from across the road.

When he told the Witness, he expected an explanation.

The Witness only asked whether the youngest had remembered his coat.

"No."

For the first time since the marks began, the keeper laughed.
