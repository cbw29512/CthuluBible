# The Second Line

The next morning the Witness returned before the tide.

He drew two lines across the black stone.

The first was close to the water. The second lay higher, where the sea reached only when the moon pulled hard upon it.

The keeper came carrying bread wrapped in cloth.

“You have made another boundary.”

“Two.”

“Yesterday you let the water erase one.”

The Witness looked toward the horizon.

“I mistook crossing for disappearance.”

The keeper sat on a dry ledge and waited.

When the tide rose, it passed the lower mark. The chalk softened beneath the water until it could no longer be seen. The upper line remained white.

The Witness stepped beyond the first line and stopped.

Nothing announced that he had crossed it.

The keeper tore the bread in half.

“Was that all?”

“No.”

“What remains?”

The Witness looked back at the place where the drowned mark had been.

“The way back.”

He did not step toward the second line.

Instead he stood until the water began to fall. When the stone emerged again, a pale trace of chalk remained in a crack.

The Witness knelt and touched it with one finger.

Then he returned to the keeper.

They ate while the tide withdrew.

Before leaving, the Witness placed the unused chalk beside the higher line.

He did not take it with him.
