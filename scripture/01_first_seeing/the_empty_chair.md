# The Empty Chair

For seven nights the Witness returned to the chamber beneath the cliff.

On the first night he placed the keeper beside the basin and wrote what the keeper saw.

On the second he stood where the keeper had stood and asked the keeper to write.

On the third they changed places whenever the lamp guttered. By dawn the tablets held two accounts of one room, agreeing in measure and differing in what each had thought worth measuring.

The keeper touched the chalk to the table.

“Then we need a witness who does not choose.”

The Witness set a third chair between them.

No one sat in it.

They arranged mirrors around the basin. They stretched threads from wall to wall. They built floats that marked the rising water without a hand upon them. They left the chamber and returned after the tide had turned.

The marks were waiting.

The keeper smiled. “There. The room has measured itself.”

The Witness studied the floats, the threads, the mirrors, and the empty chair.

“Who chose the room?”

The keeper’s smile faded.

“Who chose the tide?” the Witness asked. “Who chose what a mark should preserve, and what it may discard?”

Neither spoke for a while.

At last the keeper sat in the empty chair.

“So there is nowhere to stand outside the question.”

The Witness looked toward the black water passing beneath the stone.

“Not here.”

It was the first answer he gave that frightened the keeper.

Morning found them carrying the instruments back upstairs. One of the floats had cracked in the night. The keeper threw it toward the refuse pit.

The Witness caught it before it fell.

“Keep this one.”

“Why?”

“It failed honestly.”

They set it beside the door and went to break bread.
