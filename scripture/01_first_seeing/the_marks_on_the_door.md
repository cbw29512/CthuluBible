# The Marks on the Door

Three mornings after the woman came, the keeper found a mark drawn beside the latch.

It was only a circle crossed by a short line.

"Did you make this?" he asked.

The Witness looked at it.

"No."

The keeper scrubbed it away with seawater.

The next morning there were four.

One had been cut into the wood with a knife.

At noon a man came carrying a small jar of oil. He set it on the step and touched two fingers to the carved mark.

The keeper opened the door only far enough to stand within it.

"Who taught you that?" he asked.

The man pointed toward the harbor.

"They say this is the sign of the one who knows what comes."

Behind the keeper, the Witness said nothing.

The man lowered his head.

"My brother sails tomorrow."

The keeper glanced back.

The Witness was seated at the table, mending the same net he had once tied too tightly.

"Should he go?" the man asked.

The Witness lifted one knot, tested it between his fingers, and said, "Ask the harbor master about the wind."

The man waited.

"And the sign?"

"I did not make it."

"But does it mean anything?"

The Witness looked toward the cut in the door.

"It means someone made a mark."

The man frowned as though this answer had cost him something.

He took up his oil and left.

That evening the keeper found the Witness scraping the carved circle from the wood.

"There will be another tomorrow," he said.

"Yes."

"Then why remove this one?"

The Witness stopped.

Wood dust rested on his hand.

"Because this one is here."

By the end of the week there were marks on three boats, a fish stall, and the wall beside the public well.

Some were circles. Some had two lines. One had become an eye.

Two children had drawn theirs in mud and were jumping across it to decide who would carry the bucket home.

The keeper watched them from the doorway.

"Which is the false one?" he asked.

The Witness watched the children argue, laugh, and begin the game again.

"The one that cannot be erased."

The keeper looked at the bare place beside the latch.

In the morning, he left the bucket there.
