# The Boat That Followed

Before dawn the Witness left the headland.

He crossed alone to a black shelf of stone beyond the harbor mouth, where nothing grew and no path could be worn.

The keeper saw him there when the light came.

By midmorning a fishing boat had changed course.

Three men were in it. Their nets remained bundled beneath the forward bench.

The boat came near enough for speech.

The eldest fisherman called, "Why did you come here?"

The Witness looked at the unused nets.

"What did you leave unfinished?"

The fisherman glanced down.

"The eastern water."

"Then why are you in the western water?"

"We saw you leave."

The Witness said nothing.

The youngest man leaned over the gunwale.

"Did you mean us to follow?"

"No."

"Then why go where we could see?"

The Witness looked toward the headland, then toward the open sea.

"I had not yet learned the difference."

The eldest fisherman took up an oar.

The boat turned.

The youngest kept looking backward until the old man placed a coil of wet rope in his hands.

"Hold this."

He held it.

By the time they reached the harbor mouth, all three were facing the work inside the boat.

The Witness remained on the stone until evening.

No second boat came.

When he returned, the keeper was trimming the signal lamp.

"Was it far enough?" the keeper asked.

The Witness looked at the flame through the glass.

"No."

"How far will be?"

The Witness did not answer.

Outside, the tide covered the black shelf.

The water left no mark of his crossing.
