# The Mark Left Behind

For three tides the Witness did not return to the higher line.

On the fourth morning he came carrying a small clay cup.

The keeper found him grinding chalk against the black stone until white dust gathered in his palm. He mixed the dust with seawater and pressed his thumb into the cup, leaving a pale print inside it.

“You already know where the line is,” the keeper said.

“Yes.”

“Then why make another mark?”

The Witness set the cup on the dry ledge where the tide could not reach it.

“So that what crosses is not asked to remember everything.”

The keeper looked from the cup to the higher line.

“And if you return without remembering the hand that made it?”

The Witness was silent long enough for a wave to enter the lower chamber and withdraw.

“Then the mark may still remember the hand.”

He carried no tablet that day. He took no measuring cord. He did not move the chalk from where he had left it.

Instead he walked the path from the higher line back to the ledge several times, placing his feet in the same hollows of stone: one beside the split shell, one near the rust-colored seam, one where the floor rose toward the stairs.

The keeper watched him rehearse the return.

At last the Witness stopped beside the second line.

“Today?”

“No.”

The keeper frowned. “You have prepared everything.”

The Witness looked at the cup above the water.

“I have prepared what can be prepared.”

They climbed to the upper rooms before the tide reached the line.

The cup remained on the ledge, holding a white thumbprint and nothing else.
