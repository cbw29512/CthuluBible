# The First Question After

After they had eaten, the keeper carried the wooden bowl to the basin.

The Witness remained at the table.

The three pieces of the cup lay before him.

For a long while he said nothing.

Then he asked, "How long was I gone?"

The keeper looked toward the window.

"The lamp burned low twice. The rain stopped once. I slept, though not well."

The Witness touched the table with two fingers.

"Was that long?"

The keeper almost answered.

Instead he poured water over the bowl and watched a crumb circle the basin before sinking.

"Long enough for the bread to harden," he said.

The Witness looked at what remained of the loaf.

He pressed it with his thumb.

The crust resisted him.

"And for you?" he asked.

The keeper dried his hands.

"Long enough to be afraid you would not return."

The Witness lowered his eyes.

The keeper waited for him to speak of what he had seen.

He did not.

Instead the Witness gathered the broken clay and moved it away from the edge of the table, where an elbow might knock it to the floor.

Then he asked, "Were you hungry?"

"Yes."

"Did you eat?"

"Yes."

The Witness nodded once.

Outside, the tide had begun to fall.

Neither of them called it a return.
