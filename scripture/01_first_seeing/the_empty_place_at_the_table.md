# The Empty Place at the Table

On the fourth morning, the woman brought no question.

She brought a bowl.

The keeper set it beside the bread and reached for another chair.

"Leave it," she said.

There were already three chairs at the table.

She placed the bowl where a fourth would have sat.

The keeper looked toward the closed door.

"Is this for the Witness?"

"No."

He waited.

She broke the bread into three pieces, then a fourth. The fourth she put beside the empty bowl.

"For my eldest," she said. "If he comes home."

The keeper began to say that bread would be hard by then.

From behind the door the Witness said, "It will."

The woman looked at the door.

"Then what good is leaving it?"

There was a long silence.

At last the Witness asked, "Did he eat here before he left?"

"Every morning."

"Then the place remembers nothing. You do."

The woman frowned.

"Should I stop setting it?"

"When you no longer need the chair to be empty."

She looked at the place where no chair stood.

After a while she took the fourth piece of bread and ate it herself.

The bowl remained.

The next morning she brought three bowls.

At noon a gull came through the window and stole half a crust from the table.

The keeper cursed it.

The woman laughed until she had to sit down.

Behind the closed door, the Witness said nothing.
