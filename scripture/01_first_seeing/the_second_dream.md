# The Second Dream

A week later the keeper dreamed again.

There was no cup.

He stood on a shore he did not know. Far beyond the breakers something vast moved beneath the water, though the surface remained smooth.

A voice spoke from behind him.

He could not remember the words when he woke.

For an hour he lay still, trying to recover them.

Then he smelled smoke.

He had left the breakfast pan too near the coals.

He rose, burned his fingers moving it, swore, woke his wife, and opened the shutters.

The dream did not return.

That evening the Witness came to the headland.

The keeper waited for him to ask.

He did not.

At last the keeper said, "There was another."

The Witness watched a gull pull at something among the rocks.

"I lost what was said."

"Then it is lost."

"What if I was meant to remember?"

"By whom?"

The keeper had no answer.

"What if it mattered?"

"It may have."

The keeper looked at him sharply.

"And you would leave it lost?"

The Witness turned his gaze toward him.

"Must everything that matters become yours to keep?"

Below them, the keeper's wife shook a cloth from the doorway. Crumbs scattered into the grass. Three gulls descended at once.

The keeper laughed despite himself.

When he looked back toward the sea, he could still remember the unknown shore.

He could no longer remember why he had needed to possess it.
