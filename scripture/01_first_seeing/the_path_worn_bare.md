# The Path Worn Bare

By the seventh morning, the path to the signal house had widened.

The keeper noticed because rain no longer darkened the earth beneath the grass. Too many feet had pressed it flat.

Two brothers climbed from opposite directions.

They had not spoken since winter.

Each saw the other.

Neither stopped.

Both were looking toward the open door.

The Witness watched them pass.

When they were gone, he asked the keeper, "What was here before the path?"

"Grass."

"Before people came to the grass."

The keeper leaned on his broom.

"Boats. Nets. Work, mostly."

Near noon a fisherman climbed carrying a broken oar.

"I brought nothing," he said.

The Witness looked at the oar.

"It is broken."

"I know. I thought I would leave it here."

"Why?"

The fisherman frowned.

"Because you take no gifts."

The keeper stopped sweeping.

The Witness touched neither the man nor the wood.

"Who made it?"

"My daughter."

"Can she mend it?"

"Better than I can."

The fisherman stood for a while with the broken oar across both hands.

Then he turned downhill.

At dusk the Witness walked beyond the signal house to the last stones of the headland.

Behind him the path showed pale through the grass where many feet had pressed it bare.

Ahead there was only water.

The keeper came as far as the stones.

"Are you looking for another place?"

The Witness did not answer.

Below them, among the boats, someone called a familiar name.

Another voice answered.

Beyond the stones the water held no road.
