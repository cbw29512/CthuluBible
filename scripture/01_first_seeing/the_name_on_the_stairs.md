# The Name on the Stairs

Before dawn the keeper found the Witness beside the clay cup.

He had not touched the higher line.

Instead he had scratched one word into the stair above the reach of the tide.

The keeper bent near it. “Your name.”

“Yes.”

“You left the cup to remember your hand.”

“Yes.”

“Does the stone need to remember your name also?”

The Witness looked down toward the chamber. The sea moved below them in darkness, entering and leaving without keeping the shape of either motion.

“No.”

The keeper waited.

“I do.”

He descended to the higher line and stood with both feet behind it. Then he turned back toward the stair.

The word was small from there.

He climbed until he could touch it, descended again, and repeated the path. Each time he stopped at the same place and looked upward.

On the seventh return the keeper said, “If you come back and the word means nothing?”

The Witness rested one hand against the wall.

“Then you will speak it.”

“And if you mean nothing to me?”

The keeper had meant it lightly, but the Witness did not answer lightly.

He looked at him for a long time.

“Then do not follow me.”

The tide rose through the lower chamber and erased the first chalk line. The second remained above it.

The Witness climbed to the upper rooms.

At breakfast the keeper called his name once from across the table.

The Witness looked up.

Neither of them spoke of why.
