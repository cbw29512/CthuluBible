# The House on the Headland

Three days after the sound beneath the door, the Witness left the harbor.

The keeper went with him as far as the headland.

There stood an old signal house above the black rocks, empty since the lamp had been moved nearer the breakwater. Its roof leaked at the western corner. Salt had whitened the hinges. No other house stood within sight of it.

"Far enough?" the keeper asked.

The Witness looked back toward the harbor.

Children were running between the boats. At that distance their shouting could not be separated into words.

"For now."

They carried in a blanket, a kettle, two bowls, and the small chair from the room above the harbor.

The keeper laughed when he set down the second bowl.

"You expect company?"

"You brought it."

The keeper looked at the bowl in his hand and said nothing.

For seven days no one came.

On the eighth, the keeper climbed the path with bread and lamp oil. He stopped before the threshold.

"May I enter?"

The Witness listened before answering.

"Not today."

So they sat on opposite sides of the open doorway.

The keeper ate. The Witness did not touch the bread.

Below them, the harbor bells marked the tide.

After a while the keeper said, "They think you are angry with them."

"I am farther from them."

"They think those are the same thing."

The Witness turned his face toward the sea.

"Then they are wrong."

The keeper tore the loaf in two and pushed one half across the threshold.

The Witness looked at it for a long time.

Then he took it.

When the keeper returned to the harbor, someone asked what the Witness had taught him on the headland.

"Nothing," he said.

That night he discovered salt on his sleeve from where the two of them had leaned against the same doorframe.
