# The Fisherman Who Looked Up

The next morning the keeper carried the mended net down to the shore.

The Witness came only as far as the doorway.

A fisherman saw him there and lifted a hand in greeting.

The Witness answered.

The fisherman stopped.

His raised hand remained above his shoulder.

Behind him, gulls crossed the harbor. He followed one with his eyes until it passed before the sun.

Then he continued looking after it was gone.

The keeper called his name.

No answer.

He crossed the stones and stood between the fisherman and the doorway.

At last the fisherman blinked.

"Where did it go?" he asked.

"The gull?"

The fisherman frowned.

"No."

The keeper did not ask what he meant.

He turned toward the doorway.

The Witness was no longer there.

Inside, he found him beside the cold hearth with the shutters closed.

"He only looked at you," said the keeper.

"Yes."

"You did nothing."

The Witness was silent.

The keeper waited.

At length the Witness said, "That is what I feared."

From the shore came the ordinary noise of rope against wood and baskets being set upon stone.

The fisherman began arguing with his brother about the price of three small fish.

The keeper listened until the argument ended in laughter.

Only then did the Witness open the shutter.

Not toward the sea.

Toward the wall of the neighboring house, where someone had hung a red cloth to dry.

He watched the cloth move in the wind.

Before noon, he moved his chair farther from the door.
