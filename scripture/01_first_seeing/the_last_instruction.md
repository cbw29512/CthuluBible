# The Last Instruction

Before dawn the Witness woke the keeper.

They descended without lamps. The keeper carried the clay cup. The Witness carried nothing.

At the stair he stopped beneath the name cut into the stone.

“If I call to you,” said the keeper, “what shall I say?”

“My name.”

“And if you do not answer?”

“Say it once more.”

“And then?”

The Witness looked down toward the higher line. The tide had withdrawn beyond it, leaving the chalk whole in the dark.

“Then go upstairs.”

The keeper tightened his hand around the cup. “Without you?”

“If I cannot return to a name spoken twice, your staying will not teach me the road.”

The keeper began to object, but the Witness raised one hand.

“Keep the fire. Eat when you are hungry. Mend the west shutter before the next rain.”

“These are your instructions?”

“They are what must continue if I do not.”

The keeper looked at him for a long while.

Then he placed the clay cup on the stair beside the carved name.

“No,” he said. “It stays here.”

The Witness considered this, then inclined his head.

Together they descended to the higher line.

The keeper stopped where he had stopped before.

The Witness stepped close enough that the chalk touched the edge of his foot, but he did not cross.

Behind them, far above, the first gull began calling over the roof.

The Witness listened until it called again.

“Go upstairs,” he said.

This time the keeper obeyed.

The Witness remained beside the line until the sound of the keeper's steps had vanished.
