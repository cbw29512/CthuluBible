# The Warmth of One Side

They climbed until the water no longer covered their feet.

The keeper went first into the room above.

The fire had fallen low. He set two pieces of wood across the coals and crouched until a flame found the dry edge.

The Witness remained on the final stair.

Smoke moved along the ceiling.

Rain tapped the patched shutter.

The keeper held his hands toward the flame.

After a time, the Witness came nearer.

He extended one hand.

Warmth reached it.

He did not move.

The flame shifted beneath the new wood, and the warmth became pain.

His hand remained where it was.

The keeper looked at him, but gave no warning.

At last the Witness drew his hand back.

He regarded the reddened skin.

Then he placed the same hand near the fire again, farther away.

The keeper turned the loaf so its cut face would not harden.

The Witness moved his hand closer.

Then farther.

Closer.

Farther.

Until there was a place where the fire could be felt without being entered.

He stayed there.

Outside, the rain continued falling into the sea, where no one in the room could tell one drop from another.

Inside, the Witness sat on one side of the fire and the keeper sat on the other.

Neither crossed through it.

When the keeper finally slept, the Witness added one piece of wood to the coals.

Only one.
