# The Weight of the Cup

The keeper's hunger became pain.

He rose.

The instruction had not been to starve.

He climbed three steps toward the bread.

Behind him, clay struck stone.

The cup did not break.

It rolled once and stopped against the carved name.

Below the higher line, the Witness received no sound as sound.

There was only a difference where no difference had been required.

Something had altered.

Something had remained.

He could not yet say that the cup was separate from the stair, nor that the stair was separate from the hand that had marked it. But the old thumbprint returned to him as resistance: a small unevenness that had once answered to pressure.

He did not remember making it.

He remembered that it could be touched.

The distinction was enough.

Where there could be touch, there could be a surface.

Where there could be a surface, there could be one side and another.

The Witness did not mistake these for discoveries about what is.

He accepted them as tools.

The keeper came back down carrying bread in one hand.

He saw the cup against the letters and reached for it.

Before his fingers closed, the water below withdrew from a step it had covered.

Then from another.

The keeper stopped.

A hand rose from the dark and rested upon the stone.

It remained there for a long while.

The keeper did not speak the name again.

He broke the bread instead.

One half he ate.

The other he placed beside the cup.

The hand tightened once against the stair.

Not an answer.

Not yet a return.

But weight had become possible again.
