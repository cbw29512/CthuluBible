# The Net Mended Wrong

By noon the keeper brought the repaired net into the light.

The Witness had closed the tear with three new knots.

The keeper turned it over in his hands.

"This one is wrong," he said.

The Witness looked where he pointed.

"It holds."

"It will hold until the weight comes here."

The keeper pressed one knot with his thumb. The cord shifted against itself.

"Then it will tighten and cut the strand beside it."

The Witness watched the knot for a long time.

"Show me."

The keeper untied what the Witness had done.

He worked slowly, leaving a little play between the old cord and the new.

"You leave it loose," said the Witness.

"Enough to move."

"And that makes it stronger?"

"When the sea pulls."

The Witness took the net back.

He made the next knot as the keeper had shown him.

It was clumsy.

The keeper almost reached to correct it, then stopped.

The Witness noticed.

"Why did you stop?"

"Because you were still making it."

The Witness looked from the keeper's hand to his own.

Outside, the fishermen who had launched at dawn were returning. Their boat rode low with catch. One of them shouted toward the shore, and someone answered from among the houses.

The Witness tightened the knot, then loosened it by the smallest measure.

"Better?"

The keeper tested it.

"Better."

They repaired the rest without speaking.

When they finished, the keeper gathered the net to carry it down.

The Witness did not follow.

"You could have shown me the whole pattern," the keeper said.

The Witness looked toward the narrow window.

"You showed me one knot."

"It was enough."

The Witness touched the repaired place once.

"Then let enough remain enough."

The keeper carried the net to the shore.

That evening it returned wet, stretched, and unbroken.
