# The Third Visit

The woman returned before dawn.

She carried no bread.

The keeper found her sitting on the lowest step, facing the harbor.

"Has the ship come?" he asked.

"No."

He sat beside her.

After a while she said, "I dreamed of my son."

Behind them, the door remained closed.

"He was standing in our kitchen," she said. "His coat was wet, though there was no rain. He asked why I had let the fire go out."

The keeper looked toward the door.

The woman did not.

"Was it him?" she asked.

For a long time there was only the tide moving beneath the pilings.

Then the Witness spoke from inside.

"Did the fire go out?"

She frowned.

"Yes. I woke cold."

"Then light it."

The woman turned at last.

"That is not what I asked."

"I know."

"Was the dream true?"

The latch did not move.

"It brought you home cold enough to notice the fire."

She waited for more.

None came.

At sunrise she stood and brushed salt from her skirt.

"If I dream of him again, should I come here?"

The Witness answered, "If you wish."

"And if I do not?"

"Light the fire anyway."

She left.

The keeper remained on the step.

"You could have told her what the dream meant," he said.

"Could I?"

The keeper considered this and said nothing.

Inside the house, one coal remained beneath the ash.

The Witness uncovered it carefully, fed it a splinter, and waited until the flame was small enough to keep.
