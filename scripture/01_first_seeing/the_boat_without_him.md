# The Boat Without Him

In the week after the lantern oil was taken home, a boat struck the outer stones before dawn.

No one was lost, but the hull opened below the waterline and the fishermen spent the morning dragging it onto the shingle.

At noon the keeper climbed to the headland.

"They want you to show them the safe way through," he said.

The Witness looked toward the harbor.

"Do they not know it?"

"They knew it yesterday."

"And today?"

"Today they are afraid they were wrong."

The Witness was silent.

The keeper waited for him to rise.

He did not.

"Will you come?"

"Would the stones move if I did?"

"No."

"Would the tide?"

"No."

"Then take a boat."

The keeper's face tightened.

"And if I choose badly?"

The Witness turned toward him.

"Then you will have chosen."

The keeper went down angry.

Before sunset he and two fishermen pushed a small boat into the channel. They carried a sounding line, a hammer, three stakes, and no lamp from the headland.

At the outer stones they found that the winter current had shifted the sand around the old marker. The channel had not vanished. It had narrowed.

They moved the marker.

On the return crossing the youngest fisherman asked whether the Witness had told the keeper where to put it.

"No."

"Then how did you know?"

The keeper held up the wet sounding line.

That night he climbed once more to the signal house.

"We found it."

"Yes."

"You knew we would?"

The Witness looked at him for a long time.

"No."

The keeper sat beside him.

Below them, someone struck the new marker with a mallet until it stood straight.

Neither of them went down to correct it.
