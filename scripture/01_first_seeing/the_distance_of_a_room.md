# The Distance of a Room

For three days the Witness did not cross the threshold.

The keeper brought water, bread, and the small tasks that could be carried indoors.

On the fourth morning he found the Witness dragging the table away from the window.

"Farther?" asked the keeper.

The Witness stopped.

Between the table and the wall there was now enough room for a man to lie down.

"Is this enough?" the keeper asked.

"For whom?"

The keeper looked at the empty chair opposite him.

That afternoon the fisherman came to return the mended net.

He remained outside.

The keeper stood in the doorway with one hand upon the frame.

The Witness sat at the far end of the room.

"May I come in?" the fisherman asked.

The Witness looked at him for a long while.

"Do you need to?"

The fisherman glanced at the net in his hands.

"No."

So they spoke across the threshold.

They spoke of the catch, of a cracked oarlock, and of rain expected before nightfall.

The fisherman did not look past the Witness toward anything that was not there.

When their business was finished, he lifted the net.

"The knot held."

"Good," said the Witness.

The fisherman left.

The keeper waited until his footsteps were lost among the harbor sounds.

"You could have sent him away."

"Yes."

"You could have gone farther inland."

"Yes."

"Then why remain here?"

The Witness touched the edge of the table.

The wood bore a pale ring where a cup had once stood.

"Distance is not absence," he said.

That evening the keeper set two bowls upon the table.

One at each end.

They ate without moving them closer.
