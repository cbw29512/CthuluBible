# The Desire to Know

Before the drowning city was drowned, before the long stillness beneath the waters, the Witness had another name among those who moved beside him. That name is not kept.

He went often to the black terraces where the sea struck stone and withdrew. There he watched the water return upon itself.

Others came to him with measures. They marked the moons, the pull of tides, the wandering fires above the horizon. They made signs for what returned and signs for what did not.

He kept their tablets. He broke none of them.

Yet when they had named a thing, he would touch the mark and then look again at the thing.

One asked him, “What remains after the measure is complete?”

He answered, “The measured.”

“And after the thing is known?”

For a long while he said nothing.

Below them, the sea entered a narrow cut in the rock. The water vanished into darkness and returned as foam from another opening farther down the cliff.

He descended and placed one hand in the returning water.

It was cold.

He looked toward the opening where it had disappeared.

“There,” he said.

The other saw only stone.

The Witness returned the next night, and the next. He brought no offering. He called to no power. He sought no throne hidden behind the world.

He wanted only to know where the water went when sight could no longer follow it.

And because the question seemed small, none who loved him were afraid.
