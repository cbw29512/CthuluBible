# The Window Left Closed

Near evening the room grew warm.

The keeper rose to open the seaward window.

"Leave it," said the Witness.

The keeper stopped with his hand on the latch.

Before the crossing, the Witness had opened that window in every weather. He had said the horizon corrected rooms that had begun to believe themselves complete.

Now he sat with his back to it.

"The air is close," the keeper said.

"I know."

"Then why keep it shut?"

The Witness looked toward the glass.

Beyond it, the last light lay across the water. A gull crossed the brightness and vanished below the frame.

"Because I can still see too far."

The keeper removed his hand from the latch.

For a time they listened to the small sounds of the room: oil shifting in the lamp, wood settling near the fire, the keeper's sleeve brushing the table.

At length the Witness asked him to open the door to the passage instead.

The keeper did so.

Cool air entered from the stairwell. It smelled of wet stone and yesterday's smoke.

The Witness breathed once, slowly.

"Is that enough?" the keeper asked.

The Witness considered the question.

"For this room."

The keeper left the door open.

The window remained closed.

That night the Witness slept beside it.
