# The Remainder

In time the Witness learned every path by which the hidden water returned.

He marked the chambers beneath the cliff. He counted the breaths between disappearance and foam. He learned which moon made the passage swift and which wind pressed the sea backward into stone.

When his companions asked where the water went, he could answer them.

Still he returned.

One night the keeper of the tablets followed him down and found him kneeling beside the cut in the rock.

“You have measured this place,” the keeper said.

“Yes.”

“You have entered it.”

“Yes.”

“You have drawn what lies beneath us.”

The Witness touched the wet stone.

The keeper waited.

At last he asked, “What remains?”

The Witness pointed to the tablet beneath the keeper’s arm.

“Turn it over.”

The keeper did. The back was blank.

“Write the sea.”

“I would need another tablet.”

“Then write the tablet.”

The keeper frowned.

“And the hand that writes?” the Witness asked.

The keeper lowered the chalk.

Far inside the cliff, water struck a chamber neither of them could see. The sound arrived after the wave that made it had already changed.

The Witness listened until the echo ceased.

Then he took the tablet, not to break it, but to return it to the keeper.

“Keep the measure,” he said.

He looked toward the dark opening.

“But do not mistake its edge for the edge of what is.”
