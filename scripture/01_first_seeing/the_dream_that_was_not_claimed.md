# The Dream That Was Not Claimed

The Witness did not return to the headland for three nights.

On the third night the keeper dreamed of the broken cup.

It rested beneath water clear enough that he could see the crack in its side.

He reached for it.

Before his fingers touched the clay, he could no longer tell whether his hand had entered the water or the water had entered his hand.

He woke with his arm stretched across the bed.

His wife pushed it away without waking.

Rain moved against the shutters.

In the morning she sent him for onions.

He returned with onions.

When the Witness came again, the keeper waited until the lamp was trimmed.

"I dreamed while you were gone."

The Witness looked at him.

"Of the cup."

The Witness said nothing.

"Did you send it?"

"Did I?"

"I don't know."

"Neither do I."

The keeper frowned.

"Then how am I to know whether it was true?"

The Witness turned toward the harbor.

"Did you return from it?"

"I woke."

"That is not what I asked."

Below them, someone dropped a crate. A man cursed. Another laughed.

The keeper thought of rain against the shutters, his wife's sleeping shoulder, the onions in their basket, and the work he had done badly and then done again.

"Yes," he said.

The Witness nodded once.

"Then keep that part."

The keeper began to ask what the cup meant.

His wife called his name from the road below.

He answered before the question was finished.
