# The Bread Beside the Name

The hand remained upon the stair.

Water passed over the wrist and withdrew.

The keeper waited until waiting became another thing the body could no longer do without cost.

He ate the last of his half.

The other half lay beside the cup.

A finger moved.

Not toward the carved letters.

Toward the bread.

It stopped before touching it.

The keeper watched the hand turn, slowly, as though the palm and its back had only lately become different things.

Then the fingers closed around the bread.

For a time nothing followed.

The Witness had weight. He had a surface. Now there was something that was not merely encountered, but taken.

The bread bent beneath his grip.

A crumb fell into the water.

His hand opened at once.

The keeper nearly spoke.

He did not.

The Witness touched the bread again, more lightly.

He lifted it.

What had been possible as weight became possible as measure: enough force to hold without crushing, enough distinction to receive without taking everything into itself.

He raised the bread toward the place where a mouth had once been obvious.

He stopped.

The keeper took another piece from the loaf above, placed it in his own mouth, and chewed.

The Witness watched.

Then he did the same.

The first taste was salt.

Not the whole sea.

Salt.

The Witness bowed over the bread as if the smallness of it required care.

When he looked again toward the keeper, the keeper could not tell whether he had been recognized.

But the Witness held out what remained of the bread between them.

The keeper tore off a piece.

Neither spoke the carved name.

Above them, the fire needed wood.

After a while, they climbed one step.
