# The Morning Window

At dawn the keeper woke to the sound of the latch.

The Witness stood before the seaward window.

His hand rested upon the iron catch, but he had not lifted it.

The keeper remained where he was.

After a while the Witness said, "Yesterday I asked you to leave this closed."

"Yes."

"Do you know why?"

The keeper thought of the evening before.

"Because you could see too far."

The Witness turned his head slightly.

"That is what I said."

The keeper waited.

Outside, something struck the glass once—a moth or a bit of windblown leaf—and fell away.

"Was it true?" the keeper asked.

"Enough for yesterday."

The Witness lifted the latch.

He opened the window no wider than the breadth of two fingers.

Cold salt air entered. The flame in the lamp bent toward it and steadied.

The keeper looked past him toward the brightening sea.

"Can you bear it now?"

The Witness did not answer at once.

Far below, fishermen were pushing a boat from the stones. One slipped. Another laughed. Together they shoved until the hull floated free.

The Witness watched them instead of the horizon.

"I can bear this much," he said.

"Will you open it farther tomorrow?"

The Witness closed his eyes.

"Tomorrow is not owed an answer from today."

He left the window as it was.

Later, when the room had warmed, the keeper found him repairing a torn net beside the door.

The sea remained visible through the narrow opening.

He did not widen it.
