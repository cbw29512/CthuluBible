# The Small Bowl

From the headland, the harbor no longer assembled itself for the Witness as a village.

There were roofs, then repairs, then different roofs. Nets were cast, torn, mended, and cast again. Names changed above doors. Men argued beside a painted post until the tide covered the mark they were defending.

A funeral passed a tavern where someone laughed.

The keeper found the Witness watching from the rocks.

“You have not come down.”

The Witness turned toward the harbor.

What reached the keeper was not a sentence but a failure of scale: too many lives compressed into marks too small to hold apart.

He later wrote:

“They are difficult to see.”

The keeper looked toward the harbor. “There are hundreds of them.”

The Witness said nothing.

Below, a child carrying breakfast stumbled on the quay.

A small blue bowl struck stone and broke.

The child stared at the pieces.

Then she began to cry.

Her father knelt beside her. He did not tell her the bowl was replaceable. He gathered the pieces into his coat while she held one shard against her chest.

The Witness watched longer than he had watched the arguing men.

The keeper felt a question form around the child, the shard, and the difference between them.

“Why that one?” he wrote.

“Her mother made it.”

The Witness looked at the clay.

The keeper received continuity without shape, matter without the thing it had been.

“The clay remains.”

“Yes.”

Then loss.

“The shape does not.”

“No.”

The child was still crying.

For a moment the keeper felt the child diminish beyond measure: smaller than the harbor, smaller than the coast, smaller than any scale by which a star might notice a world.

He recoiled.

Then that same shrinking passed through the Witness.

The keeper wrote the two impressions as:

“She is almost nothing.”

And after a long silence:

“From far enough away, so am I.”

The Witness descended the path.

At the quay, the child had dropped one piece beneath a cart wheel.

The Witness reached for it.

His hand could have closed around the shard and made powder of it.

It did not.

He placed the piece in the child’s palm.

She did not bow.

She did not ask him what it meant.

She wiped her face with her sleeve and ran after her father.

The Witness remained beside the broken place in the road until someone shouted at him to move because a fish cart was coming.

He moved.
