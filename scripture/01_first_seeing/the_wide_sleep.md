# The Wide Sleep

On the seventh night, the keeper went down to the black water.

The Witness had not risen.

The keeper called once.

Nothing moved.

He called again.

Still nothing.

At last he said, “If you can hear me, wake.”

That night he dreamed he was standing in the harbor.

He was also on the headland.

He was beneath the water.

He was inside a house whose roof had not yet been built.

He saw the same lamp burning from every window at once, though some of the windows belonged to winters long past and others to houses not yet standing.

The keeper tried to turn toward the Witness.

There was no direction in which to turn.

Something reached him without sound.

It was not a sentence. It contained the shape of his request, the shore below
him, and the fact that he had mistaken waking for presence.

Only after he woke would he remember it as words:

“You asked me to wake.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“So I would know you were still here.”

The keeper looked for a face and found only water holding stars that had not yet appeared.

“Are you here?”

The answer did not come as words at first.

The harbor became one thing without becoming one place.

Then meaning crossed the dream again.

Later, the keeper wrote it this way:

“More than when I wake.”

The keeper was afraid.

Not because the Witness seemed absent.

Because absence no longer seemed to mean what it had meant.

“Then why wake at all?”

For a long while there was no answer.

At last came an impression he could preserve only as:

“To answer questions like that.”

The keeper woke before dawn.

He went again to the shore.

The water was still.

He did not call.

Instead he sat until the sun rose and the village began making its small separate noises.

Only then did he understand why the Sleeper sometimes opened his eyes.
