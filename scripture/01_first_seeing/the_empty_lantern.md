# The Empty Lantern

For three nights the lamp on the headland remained dark.

On the fourth morning the keeper carried the lantern down to the harbor to fill it with oil.

A fisherman stopped him beside the net sheds.

"Has he gone?"

"No."

"Then why is there no light?"

The keeper looked at the lantern in his hand.

"Because we found the channel."

The fisherman frowned.

"What if we lose it again?"

"Then we will sound the bell."

"And if no one answers?"

The keeper had no answer for that.

He filled the lantern and climbed back to the headland.

The Witness was sitting outside the signal house where the sea could be heard but not seen.

The keeper set the full lantern between them.

"They think darkness means you have left."

"Have I?"

"No."

"Then darkness means something else."

The keeper waited.

The Witness did not tell him what.

At dusk a bell sounded below.

The keeper reached for the lantern.

Before he touched it, another bell answered from across the harbor.

Then a third.

His hand remained on the handle.

"I filled it for nothing," he said.

The Witness looked at the lantern.

"You filled it because you might need it."

"Is that different?"

"Yes."

They sat until the bells ceased.

The lantern remained full.

In the morning the keeper carried it down again, not because it was empty, but because his wife needed the oil for the kitchen lamp.

The Witness watched him go and did not call him back.
