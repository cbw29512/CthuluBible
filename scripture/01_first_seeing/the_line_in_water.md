# The Line in Water

For seven mornings the Witness did not return to the room beneath the stair.

He went instead to the eastern shelf where the tide withdrew across black stone. There he carried no tablets. He took only a length of chalk.

At low water he drew a white line around a shallow pool.

The keeper watched from above.

“What does it measure?”

“Nothing.”

The tide returned. Water crossed the chalk, blurred it, and lifted part of it away. When the sea withdrew again, the Witness drew another line where the pool now ended.

On the second morning he did the same.

On the third, the keeper said, “The water does not care where you put the boundary.”

“No.”

“Then why keep drawing it?”

The Witness looked at the small creatures stranded in the pool. One clung beneath a ledge where the sun could not reach it.

“Because I do.”

The keeper frowned.

The Witness touched the chalk to stone, but did not draw.

“I have been trying to find a place from which nothing is divided by the one who looks. Perhaps that is the wrong labor.”

“What is the right one?”

He watched the next thin sheet of water enter the pool. For an instant the old chalk line could still be seen beneath it.

“To learn whether a boundary may be crossed without pretending it was never useful.”

The keeper picked up the broken piece of chalk.

Together they waited until the tide covered their feet.

When they climbed back to the house, neither marked where the sea ended and the land began.
