# The Sound Beyond the Door

In the weeks after the coat was returned, people came more often to the house.

Some brought bread. Some brought questions. Some brought nothing and stood in the road until the keeper told them to go home.

The Witness kept the door closed.

One night the keeper woke to voices outside.

A dozen people had gathered beneath the window. No one was speaking loudly. They were listening.

"To what?" the keeper asked.

A fisherman raised one finger.

From behind the door came a sound too low to be called a voice. It seemed at first like water moving under stone. Then the keeper thought he heard his mother's singing, though she had been dead many years.

The woman with the coat heard gulls over a harbor she had never visited.

Another man began to weep.

The keeper opened the outer door and found the Witness seated on the floor of his room, both hands pressed against his mouth.

"Are you calling them?" he asked.

"No."

Outside, someone whispered that the sound had changed.

The Witness looked toward the wall as though the wall were very thin.

"Then stop it," said the keeper.

"I am trying."

The keeper had never heard fear in his voice before.

He shut the outer door and sent the people away. Some protested. The woman did not. She took the weeping man by the arm and led him down toward the lamps of the harbor.

Before dawn the sound ceased.

The keeper found the Witness standing at the window, though the shutter remained closed.

"You did nothing," the keeper said.

"Not enough."

"What will you do?"

For a long time the Witness listened to the ordinary sea.

"Go farther."

At sunrise the fishermen pushed their boats into the water.

The Witness did not come out to watch them.
