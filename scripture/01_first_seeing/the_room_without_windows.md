# The Room Without Windows

After the empty chair, the Witness stopped asking how to make an instrument that did not choose.

Instead he asked what remained when every choice he could name was taken away.

He built a room beneath the lowest stair. It had no windows. Its walls were cut from one stone. He brought no mirror, no tablet, no clock, no vessel for the tide. At the threshold the keeper found him removing even the lamp.

“How will you record what happens?”

“I will not.”

“How will you compare it?”

“I will not.”

The keeper held out the cracked float they had saved. “Then take this.”

The Witness turned it once in his hand and gave it back.

“That remembers the water for me.”

He entered, and the keeper closed the door.

For a long while there was only stone, breath, and the faint pressure of the sea beyond the wall.

The Witness tried to attend without naming. Each time a distinction arose—near and far, silence and sound, the one who listened and the thing heard—he let it pass without following it.

But there was always another distinction beneath it.

At last he opened the door.

The keeper had fallen asleep against the wall. The cracked float rested in his lap.

“Did you find the place outside the question?” the keeper asked, waking.

The Witness looked back into the dark room.

“No.”

The keeper waited.

“I found how much of the question I carry with me.”

They went upstairs before dawn. Neither spoke of trying again.

At breakfast the keeper salted the bread twice by mistake. The Witness ate it without correction.
