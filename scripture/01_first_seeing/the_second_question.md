# The Second Question

The fisherman came again three days later.

This time he brought his sister.

She carried no rope.

The keeper saw them from the steps and asked what had broken.

"Nothing," said the fisherman. "She has a question."

The door was closed.

His sister stood before it for a long while.

At last she said, "My son has gone north on a merchant ship. Will he return?"

No answer came.

She stepped nearer.

"You knew where the brace should go."

From behind the door the Witness said, "No."

The woman waited.

"No, he will not return?"

"No. I knew where a brace might hold because your brother told me where the wood had split."

She put one hand against the door.

"But you have seen farther than we have."

The keeper turned toward the harbor.

Beyond the roofs, a mast moved slowly between the breakwaters and vanished.

"Farther is not the same as there," said the Witness.

The woman's hand remained on the wood.

"Then what should I do?"

The Witness did not answer at once.

A gull struck a shell against the stones. Somewhere below, someone cursed at a tangled line.

"What did you do before you came here?"

"I baked bread."

"Is it finished?"

She looked at her brother.

"No."

"Then your oven is waiting."

The woman lowered her hand.

She did not thank the door.

She went home.

That evening she returned with a small loaf and left it on the step.

The keeper carried it inside and set it before the Witness.

"Was that an offering?" he asked.

The Witness broke the loaf in two.

"It was bread."

He gave half to the keeper.

The other half they ate before it cooled.
