# The Third Dream

The keeper dreamed of the harbor with no boats in it.

The piers remained. Ropes knocked softly against empty posts. Lamps burned in windows where no one moved.

Across the water stood the Witness.

The keeper called to him.

The Witness raised one hand, not in greeting, but as if to stop him from stepping forward.

Then the tide rose without wind.

The keeper woke with his feet on the floor.

His wife was awake beside him.

"Where are you going?"

He looked toward the dark window.

"I don't know."

She waited.

After a while he put his feet back beneath the blanket.

In the morning he told the Witness what had happened.

"You stopped me," the keeper said.

"Did I?"

"I saw you."

"You saw something wearing a shape you knew."

The keeper frowned. "Then I should distrust it?"

"I did not say that."

"Trust it?"

"I did not say that either."

The keeper rubbed sleep from one eye.

"What am I supposed to do with a thing like that?"

The Witness looked toward the harbor. Two children were arguing over a coil of rope while their father pretended not to hear.

"What did you do?"

"I went back to bed."

"And afterward?"

"I woke. I patched the north shutter. I ate. I came here."

The Witness turned back to the sea.

The keeper waited for judgment.

None came.

Below them, one child surrendered the rope. The other immediately gave half of it back.

The keeper watched until he forgot to ask whether the dream had been a warning.
