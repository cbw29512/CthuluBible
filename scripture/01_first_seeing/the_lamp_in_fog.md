# The Lamp in Fog

The fog came before sunset and covered the headland.

From the harbor the signal house disappeared first, then the rocks beneath it. At last even the lamp became only a pale stain where the hill had been.

The keeper climbed with a covered lantern and found the Witness beside the shutter.

"They cannot see you tonight," he said.

"No."

"Then the lamp is useless."

The Witness opened the shutter a finger's width.

Below them the fog brightened, but no beam crossed it. The light entered the white air and was lost.

The keeper watched for a while.

"You could make it brighter."

"Yes."

"Would they see it then?"

The Witness did not answer.

Far below, a bell sounded from the harbor mouth.

Once.

Then again, farther to the east.

The keeper turned his head.

"They are sounding for one another."

The Witness closed the shutter until the lamp was hidden.

The bell sounded a third time.

Another answered from within the harbor.

"Now they have less," said the keeper.

"Less of me."

They listened.

Through the fog came the scrape of an oar, a man's voice calling depth, and another voice answering. Neither voice called toward the headland.

The keeper smiled.

"They found the channel."

The Witness sat beside the cold hearth.

After a time the keeper asked, "Will you light it tomorrow?"

"If it helps them return."

"And if it makes them climb?"

The Witness looked toward the shuttered window.

"Then it is too much light."

Near dawn the fog thinned.

The keeper opened the door and saw the harbor lamps below, each small and separate.

He left the signal lamp dark.

The Witness did not correct him.
