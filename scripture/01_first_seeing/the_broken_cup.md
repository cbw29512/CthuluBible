# The Broken Cup

Near dawn, the keeper woke to the sound of clay striking stone.

The cup lay in three pieces beside the hearth.

The Witness was crouched over it.

The keeper rose and reached for the broom.

The Witness stopped his hand.

He lifted one piece of the cup and turned it between his fingers.

The old thumbprint remained in the clay.

He set the piece down.

Then he lifted another.

No mark.

The keeper waited.

At last he said, "Is it still the cup?"

The Witness looked toward the stair where his name had been cut into stone.

Then toward the bread.

Then toward the sleeping fire.

"Which piece?" he asked.

"All of it."

The Witness gathered the three pieces into his palm.

They fit together badly. A narrow darkness remained between two edges.

"You have changed the question," he said.

The keeper frowned.

The Witness placed the marked piece in the keeper's hand and carried the other two to the table.

He did not mend them.

When morning came, the keeper poured water into a wooden bowl instead.

The broken cup remained beside it.

Before they ate, the Witness touched the old thumbprint once.

Then he withdrew his hand.

He ate from the bread that had not been broken yesterday.
