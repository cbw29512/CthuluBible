# The Place Beside Him

The woman climbed the headland carrying nothing.

She found the Witness standing beside the open door of the signal house. The keeper was inside, scraping salt from the hinges.

She stopped three steps below them.

"My son came home," she said.

The Witness looked toward the harbor.

"Yes."

She lowered herself to her knees.

The Witness moved aside.

The woman looked up.

There was no one before her now, only the doorway and, beyond it, the keeper with a knife in his hand and rust on his sleeve.

She turned.

"Why did you move?"

"You were facing me."

"I know."

"Why?"

Her mouth opened, then closed.

Below them a boy shouted from the path.

"Mother!"

She looked back. Her son was climbing with a coil of rope over one shoulder.

"I wanted to thank you," she said.

"For his return?"

"Yes."

"Did I bring his boat?"

"No."

"Did I calm the water?"

"No."

She looked down at her hands.

"Then what am I thanking you for?"

The Witness did not answer.

Her son reached the steps, breathing hard.

"You said you would help with the nets."

"I did."

"You forgot."

"I did."

She stood.

Before leaving, she looked once at the place where the Witness had been.

"Should no one kneel there?"

The Witness looked at the worn stone.

"People will."

That evening the keeper swept the doorway.

By morning the wind had put sand there again.

He stepped across it carrying a bucket and did not notice.
