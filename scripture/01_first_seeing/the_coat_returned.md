# The Coat Returned

Near evening, a sailor came to the house carrying a folded coat.

The woman knew it before he spoke.

She took it from him with both hands.

The sailor removed his cap. He told her where the storm had taken the boat, and where they had searched afterward.

He did not say that they would search again.

When he had gone, the woman sat beside the cold hearth with the coat across her knees.

The keeper brought a lamp.

She pressed one sleeve to her face.

"It still smells of him," she said.

Behind the closed door, the Witness was silent.

After a time she asked, "Is this what is left?"

The keeper looked toward the door.

No answer came.

The woman lowered the sleeve.

"No," she said. "This is his coat."

She wept then, and neither man interrupted her.

Winter came early that year.

One morning her youngest son arrived wearing the coat. The sleeves covered his hands, and one shoulder had been patched with cloth that did not match.

The keeper stared at him.

"Your brother's?"

The boy nodded.

His mother had shortened it badly. One cuff was higher than the other.

The keeper waited until the boy had gone to fetch water.

"Did she give him away with it?" he asked.

From behind the door the Witness said, "No."

Outside, the boy tripped over the hem and swore.

His mother called after him to mind his language.

The keeper laughed.

After a moment, so did she.
