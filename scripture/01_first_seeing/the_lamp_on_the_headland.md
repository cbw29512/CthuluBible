# The Lamp on the Headland

On the twelfth night the keeper saw the old signal lamp burning again.

No ship had asked for it. The harbor lamp was nearer the channel and brighter.

Still, the small light remained above the black rocks until dawn.

The next evening the keeper climbed the path with oil.

"You lit it," he said.

The Witness was seated beside the cold hearth.

"Yes."

"For the ships?"

"No."

The keeper waited.

At last he asked, "Then for whom?"

The Witness looked toward the harbor. From the headland the windows were only yellow points beneath the dark.

"For those who look back."

The keeper set the oil beside the door.

"They may come if they see it."

"They may know where I am without coming here."

That night three figures appeared on the lower path.

The Witness saw them before the keeper did.

He rose and covered the lamp with his hand.

The figures stopped.

For a moment the light shone red between his fingers.

Then he lowered the shutter until only a narrow line remained.

The three figures stood together in the dark. None climbed farther.

After a while one raised a hand toward the house.

The Witness raised his own.

They turned back toward the harbor.

The keeper watched their lantern descend the path.

"You could have opened the door."

"Yes."

"You wanted them to know you were here."

"Yes."

"But not to reach you."

The Witness looked at the thin line of light beneath the shutter.

"Not every light is an invitation."

Before dawn the oil ran low.

The keeper trimmed the wick.

Neither of them made it brighter.
