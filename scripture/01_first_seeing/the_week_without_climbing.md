# The Week Without Climbing

For seven days the keeper did not climb the headland.

On the first day he repaired the harbor marker.

On the second he helped patch the boat that had struck the stones.

On the third his wife sent him for onions, and he returned with turnips.

On the fourth it rained.

On the fifth he thought of the path to the signal house and chose another street.

On the sixth he did not think of it at all.

On the seventh, near evening, he climbed.

The Witness was sitting outside the signal house with the shuttered lamp behind him.

The keeper stopped at the top of the path.

"You did not call me."

"No."

"Did you know I would come?"

"No."

The keeper looked back toward the harbor.

"Were you waiting?"

The Witness considered this.

"I was here."

The keeper sat beside him.

After a while he said, "I thought perhaps I ought to come."

"Why?"

"Because you are here."

The Witness said nothing.

"That is not a reason, is it?"

"It is a reason."

"A good one?"

"For what?"

The keeper laughed once through his nose.

Below them, the evening bell sounded from the harbor.

"I came because I wanted to," he said.

The Witness turned toward him.

"Then stay until you want to go."

They watched the first boats come in.

When the keeper stood, the Witness did not ask when he would return.

Halfway down the path the keeper remembered the onions.

This time he went back for them.
