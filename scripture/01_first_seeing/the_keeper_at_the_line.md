# The Keeper at the Line

That evening the keeper descended alone.

The Witness found him standing before the higher line, one foot lifted above the chalk.

“Put it down,” said the Witness.

The keeper set his foot behind the mark.

“You have crossed it in your thoughts every day,” he said. “Why may I not cross it with you?”

The Witness looked toward the dark water.

“Because I asked the question.”

“And I cannot?”

“You can.”

The keeper frowned. “Then why stop me?”

The Witness touched the name cut into the stair above them.

“Ask your own question.”

For a while there was only the tide moving through the chamber.

The keeper looked again at the line. “What if mine leads to the same place?”

“Then it will not need my footsteps.”

“And if I choose yours?”

The Witness was silent long enough that the keeper lowered his eyes.

“At least know that you chose it.”

They climbed together.

At the upper door the keeper turned back. From there the chalk could no longer be seen, though both knew where it lay.

He said, “Will you tell me before you go?”

“Yes.”

“Will you tell me what you expect to find?”

“No.”

“Because it would harm me?”

The Witness opened the door.

“Because I do not know.”

Rain had begun outside. They set bowls beneath two leaks in the roof and moved the bread away from the wall.

Through the night, water gathered drop by drop in both bowls.
