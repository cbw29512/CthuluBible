# The Gifts on the Steps

The next time the keeper climbed the headland, three things lay on the lowest step of the signal house.

A loaf wrapped in cloth.

A little copper fish.

A jar of oil with a blue cord tied around its neck.

The keeper lifted the fish.

"They are thanking you."

The Witness looked at the harbor.

"For what?"

"The channel. The boats. The things they think you have done."

"Did I move the marker?"

"No."

"Did I patch the hull?"

"No."

The keeper set the fish down.

"They still want to give you something."

The Witness picked up the loaf.

For a moment the keeper thought he might eat it.

Instead he placed it in the keeper's hands.

"Mara's house," he said.

The keeper frowned. "Her husband is still at sea."

"Yes."

The Witness gave him the jar of oil.

"The midwife."

"Her lamp broke last winter."

"Her new one did not."

The keeper looked at the copper fish.

"And this?"

The Witness did not touch it.

"Who made it?"

"Tomas, I think. His daughter works the metal stall."

"Then ask him."

The keeper carried the bread and oil down the hill.

The next morning he returned with the copper fish still in his pocket.

"Tomas said his daughter made it after her brother came home. He said it belongs here."

The Witness looked at the small fish.

"Does her brother have one?"

"No."

The keeper laughed.

He took the fish back down the hill.

After that, people sometimes left things on the steps.

Nothing remained there long.

Years later, some said the Witness accepted no gifts.

Others said every gift given to him was accepted.

The keeper, when asked which was true, said he had once eaten half a loaf on the way to Mara's house because he had missed breakfast.
