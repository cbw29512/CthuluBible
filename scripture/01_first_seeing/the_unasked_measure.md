# The Unasked Measure

After this, the Witness began to measure not only the water, but the measuring.

He set two bowls beneath the same fall and marked the time required to fill each. When one filled sooner, he changed the bowls. When the difference remained, he changed the marks. When the marks agreed, he changed the hand that made them.

The keeper of the tablets watched until the lamps burned low.

“What error are you hunting?” the keeper asked.

The Witness looked at the rows of figures.

“The one that asks the question.”

The keeper laughed once, believing this was a riddle.

The Witness did not laugh.

He moved one lamp from the left side of the table to the right. Shadows crossed the wet stone. The numbers did not change, but the room did.

“Does the water know where we stand?” asked the keeper.

“I do not know.”

“Then why count our footsteps?”

“Because we are present in every count.”

The keeper looked again at the tablets. For the first time he saw that each bore, beside its measures of tide and moon, the unnoticed history of the one who had measured: where the hand had rested, which sound had been ignored, what had been chosen as beginning.

He reached for the chalk.

The Witness stopped him.

“Not yet.”

Outside, someone called them to eat.

The keeper glanced toward the passage.

“Will you come?”

The Witness covered the tablets with cloth.

“Yes.”

They left the chamber together, while beneath the cliff the water continued through darkness without waiting to be observed.
