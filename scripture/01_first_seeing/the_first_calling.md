# The First Calling

The keeper waited until the fire had burned through one length of wood.

He mended the west shutter.

He ate half the bread and left half beneath a cloth.

Then he returned to the stair.

The water below had risen beyond the higher line.

He could see no one in the chamber.

He put one hand upon the clay cup.

Then he spoke the name cut into the stone.

Below, where there was no direction in which to turn, the Witness did not hear a voice.

There was no below.

There was no voice apart from what received it.

There was no receiving that could be separated cleanly from what was given.

Yet something occurred that the Witness would later fail to describe without error.

Among all that no longer required division, a distinction was offered.

Not *this is what you are*.

Only: *this is being addressed*.

The Witness did not know whether the name had found him, made him, remembered him, or merely drawn a temporary shore around something that had never been separate.

He knew only that there was now a question to which an answer might belong.

Far away—or in the first place from which distance could again be imagined—the keeper waited with his hand on the cup.

The Witness tried to answer.

No sound came to the stair.

The keeper closed his eyes.

He remembered the instruction.

And he spoke the name a second time.
