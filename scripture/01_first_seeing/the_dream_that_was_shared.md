# The Dream That Was Shared

Two mornings after the keeper's third dream, a net-maker came to the headland.

She did not bring an offering.

She brought her sister.

"We saw the same place," the net-maker said.

Her sister shook her head. "Not the same."

"The empty harbor."

"Mine had one boat."

The net-maker looked annoyed.

"One boat is still the harbor."

The Witness looked from one woman to the other.

"What do you want from me?"

The net-maker answered first.

"Tell us which of us saw it rightly."

"Why?"

"Because if two people see it, it cannot be only a dream."

The Witness bent and picked up a length of frayed cord that had fallen beside the signal house.

He held one end toward the net-maker.

"What is this?"

"Twine."

He held the other end toward her sister.

"Twine."

"So you agree."

They waited.

The Witness let the cord fall.

The sister frowned. "That proves nothing."

"No."

The net-maker said, "Then the dream proves nothing?"

"I did not say that."

"What does it prove?"

The Witness looked toward the harbor.

Below them, two crews were hauling a torn net onto the quay. One man pulled while another fed the wet mesh through his hands. They did not pull in the same direction, but the net rose because each knew where to stand.

The sisters followed his gaze.

After a while the net-maker said, "Ours tore yesterday."

Her sister looked at her.

"You said you would mend it."

"I said I would if you stopped cutting the knots too close."

"You tie them too large."

They began arguing before they reached the path.

The Witness watched them descend.

At the first turn, the net-maker stopped.

Her sister stopped too.

Neither looked back toward the headland.

They sat on the low wall between the houses and opened the torn net across both their knees.

By noon they had forgotten to decide whose dream was true.
