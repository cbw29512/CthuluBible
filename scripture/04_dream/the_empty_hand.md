# The Empty Hand

Among those who called for the Sleeper to wake was a man who had ceased to trust the shape of doors.

He had dreamed too deeply and returned badly.

His companions called this advancement.

They gathered at the shore and asked the black water:

“How did you obtain what you have obtained?”

That night the man dreamed he was climbing a stair.

At the first landing he was told to leave his silver.

He left it.

At the second he was told to leave his name.

He left it.

At the third he was told to leave every face by which he had ever known himself.

He wept, but left them.

At the fourth there was no voice.

Only an open hand.

So he opened his own.

Nothing lay in it.

He felt joy.

At last, he thought, I have nothing.

The hand before him closed.

His own remained open.

Then he understood that he was still holding something.

He woke before he could say what.

At dawn his companions surrounded him.

“What did the Sleeper teach you?”

The man looked at his empty palm.

For a long time he said nothing.

Then one of them whispered:

“Was the answer nothing?”

The man closed his hand.

From beneath the water came no reply.
