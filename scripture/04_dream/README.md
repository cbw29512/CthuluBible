# 04 — The Book of the Dream

In progress.

Scope: hymns, visions, parables, and contemplative material exploring the Dream
and the Flood — how contact with what exceeds finite perception is translated
into survivable forms.

## Transmission rule

Nothing in this book should be treated as a verbatim message from Cthulhu.

The Sleeper's mature mode of contact is not human speech. Dreamers receive
images, impossible spatial relations, temporal overlap, intuitions, absences,
pressures, symbols, and partial structures that the waking mind later converts
into language.

Two people may receive related contact and preserve different sentences. The
difference may belong to the translators rather than the source.

Therefore:

- quotation marks are a literary convenience, not proof of literal speech;
- no pronounceable "true language of Cthulhu" is canonical;
- visions should become less certain when forced into exact wording;
- the strongest dream teaching may survive as an image whose meaning cannot be
  completely recovered;
- named human religions should not be invoked inside the scripture to announce
  parallels; their structural echoes remain invisible.

The book should repeatedly show human beings turning fragments into gods,
doctrines, moral personalities, sacred identities, or final answers — and then
showing those constructions fail to contain what prompted them.
