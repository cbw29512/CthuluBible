# The Map That Showed Everything

A surveyor dreamed that a stranger placed a map upon his table.

Every road was drawn correctly.

So were the footpaths.

So were the stones beside the footpaths, the roots beneath the stones, the tunnels of insects beneath the roots, and the cracks inside the grains of sand.

The surveyor bent closer.

He found the room in which he slept.

He found the table.

He found the map upon the table.

On the map within the map he found the same roads, stones, roots, tunnels, cracks, room, table, and map.

Nothing had been omitted.

He woke with his hands gripping the blanket.

For seven days he tried to redraw what he had seen.

His first copy filled a wall.

His second filled every wall in the house.

By the third, he had stopped marking roads because there was no room left between the smaller things.

A traveler came to him and asked the way to the northern bridge.

The surveyor opened his drawings.

He showed the traveler the shape of the riverbed, the mineral seams beneath it, the nests in the eastern bank, and a fracture inside one stone of the bridge.

"Which road?" the traveler asked.

The surveyor searched.

The road was there.

He could no longer find it.

That night he dreamed again.

This time a different hand placed a small scrap of paper upon the table.

It showed one road, one bend, the river, and a mark where the bridge stood.

The surveyor became angry.

"This leaves almost everything out."

No answer came.

In the morning the traveler was still waiting.

The surveyor looked at the walls.

Then he drew the small map from memory.

The traveler reached the bridge before noon.

The surveyor did not burn the larger maps.

Neither did he give them to travelers.
