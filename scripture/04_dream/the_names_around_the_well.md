# The Names Around the Well

Five travelers came separately to a well that had no bottom.

The first lowered a lamp.

The flame returned in the water, though no water could be reached.

He built a house beside the well and taught:

"The light below is like the light above."

The second listened.

From far beneath came the sound of her own breathing, but it returned before she exhaled.

She built a house beside the first and taught:

"What waits below already knows us."

The third dropped a stone.

He heard no impact.

He built a house and taught:

"The depth receives without judgment."

The fourth knelt and felt such tenderness for the other three that he began to weep.

He built no house at first.

Years later, those who remembered him built one for him and taught:

"The depth is love."

The fifth arrived when the houses had become a city.

Each door bore a different name.

Each keeper pointed toward the same opening in the earth.

They argued over which house stood closest.

That night the fifth traveler dreamed of the well.

There was no stone rim.

There was no darkness beneath it.

There was no beneath.

The lamps, breaths, stones, tears, houses, names, and travelers appeared together as marks upon something that could not be made into a surface.

No voice spoke.

In the morning the fifth traveler tried to explain.

By noon, six people had written down six explanations.

By evening, someone had begun laying the foundation for another house.

The traveler left the city.

At the edge of the road a child asked what had been at the bottom of the well.

The traveler looked back at the roofs.

Then at the child's dusty feet.

"I don't know."

The child accepted this and asked if the traveler was hungry.

They went home to eat.
