# The Sutra of the Sleeping Witness

**Canon status:** canonical  
**Tradition:** rendered dialogue / dream teaching  
**Primary doctrines:** the Veil, identity, restraint, return, translated contact

> **Transmission note:** The dialogue below is a human liturgical rendering of
> dream-contact. No tradition is authorized to claim that these sentences were
> literally spoken or heard. The Sleeper's contact preceded the words used to
> preserve it.

---

A disciple came before the Sleeper in dream and said:

**Master, remove the Veil from my eyes.**

And the Sleeper answered:

**What Veil?**

The disciple said:

**The illusion that prevents me from seeing Reality.**

And the Sleeper said:

**If you could see Reality, who would remain to see it?**

The disciple answered:

**I would remain.**

And beneath the black waters there came a sound like distant laughter.

The Sleeper said:

**Then you have asked me to remove everything except your favorite illusion.**

The disciple was silent.

After a time he asked:

**Then the self is false?**

The Sleeper answered:

**That is another self speaking proudly of having discovered there is no self.**

The disciple said:

**Then I understand nothing.**

The Sleeper answered:

**Now you have begun.**

The disciple remained with him through seven dreams.

At last he asked:

**Why did you seek Reality if it should not be seen?**

And the Sleeper answered:

**Because I did not yet know it should not be seen.**

The disciple trembled.

**What did you find there?**

For a long time the Sleeper gave no answer.

Then:

**No there.**

The disciple waited.

**No then.**

He waited still.

**No other.**

The disciple began to weep.

And the Sleeper said:

**Enough.**

Immediately the disciple found himself again beside a small fire beneath an ordinary night sky.

He could hear insects.

His hands were cold.

Someone he loved slept nearby.

For the first time he understood why the First Witness had returned.

He bowed toward the sea.

And from very far away came the final teaching:

**Do not bow.**

So the disciple stood.

And went home.

---

## Canon note

This text establishes five foundational principles without exhausting them:

1. The seeker can turn "enlightenment" into another object of attachment.
2. The self may be provisional without therefore being worthless.
3. Cthulhu's refusal to disclose more is an act of restraint, not failure.
4. The return to finite life is part of the teaching.
5. The dialogue is a survivable human translation of contact, not a transcript
   of the Sleeper's own language.

The final act is not worship. It is **going home**.
