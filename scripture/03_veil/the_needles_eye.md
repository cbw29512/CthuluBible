# The Needle's Eye

A student brought a torn coat to the old weaver and found her twisting thread between wet fingers.

“Why do you make it smaller?” he asked.

“So it will pass.”

She pressed the loose fibers to a point and guided them through the eye of the needle.

The student frowned. “But the thread is wider than that.”

“Yes.”

“Then the needle admits only part of it.”

“No.”

She drew the thread through and held it up.

“What passed?” she asked.

“The thread.”

“What was left behind?”

The student looked at the floor. There were only a few loose fibers from where he had handled it badly.

He said, “Nothing that mattered to the stitch.”

The weaver shook her head.

“Do not become proud so quickly. Those fibers were also thread.”

She began closing the tear.

The student watched the cloth draw together.

After a while he said, “Then the eye lies about the size of the thread.”

“The eye says nothing.”

“It refuses what cannot pass.”

“It has no mouth with which to refuse.”

He considered this.

“Then why not make the eye wider?”

The weaver handed him a sailmaker's needle with a broad opening.

He pushed it through the coat. The cloth split around it.

The weaver took back the needle.

“For this cloth,” she said, “that was too much.”

The student looked at the small needle, then at the tear.

“Which eye shows the thread as it truly is?”

The weaver laughed once.

Then she put the small needle into his hand.

“Your sleeve is still open.”

He threaded it badly the first time.

On the second attempt, enough passed.
