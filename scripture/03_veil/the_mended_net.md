# The Mended Net

A teacher took a student to the shore before sunrise.

The fishermen were repairing a net.

The student watched them tie one broken cord to another.

“If the net were perfect,” he said, “nothing would pass through it.”

The teacher asked a fisherman for a scrap of sailcloth.

Together they stretched the cloth between two poles and lowered it into the water.

When they tried to draw it back, the sea held it.

The poles bent. One snapped.

The fisherman laughed and returned to his net.

The student said, “Then the holes are necessary.”

The teacher shook his head.

A little farther down the beach lay another net whose cords had rotted through. Fish passed between gaps wider than a hand.

“Those holes are necessary too?”

“No.”

The teacher sat beside the fishermen.

For a while they repaired the net without speaking.

At last the student asked, “How large should the openings be?”

One of the fishermen held up the wooden gauge he used for tying the mesh.

“This large.”

“Why?”

“Because of what we are trying to catch.”

The student looked toward the dark water.

“And if we wished to catch the sea?”

The fisherman handed him the broken pole.

The student carried it home for firewood.
