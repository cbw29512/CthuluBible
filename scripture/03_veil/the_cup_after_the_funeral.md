# The Cup After the Funeral

After the burial, a woman remained at the table while the others carried the benches outside.

Before her sat her brother's cup.

She asked the teacher:

“Where is he now?”

The teacher touched the cup.

“Is this your brother?”

“No.”

He pointed to the chair where her brother had sat.

“Is that?”

“No.”

He asked her to speak her brother's name.

She did.

“Is the name your brother?”

“No.”

“What you remember?”

She hesitated.

“No. I forget things.”

“The body beneath the hill?”

The woman began to weep.

“It was his.”

The teacher waited.

At last she said:

“You have taken him away piece by piece.”

“No.”

She looked at him angrily.

“You have shown me that nothing here is him.”

The teacher shook his head.

“I showed you that everything you pointed to was not enough.”

The woman looked again at the cup.

Her brother had chipped its rim years before. She remembered laughing when he denied it.

She began to laugh now, though she was still crying.

“Does that mean some part of him remains?”

The teacher did not answer.

“Does it mean he is gone?”

Again the teacher did not answer.

Outside, someone called for help carrying the last bench.

The woman wiped her face.

She took her brother's cup to the basin, washed it, and placed it among the others.

Then she went outside and lifted one end of the bench.
